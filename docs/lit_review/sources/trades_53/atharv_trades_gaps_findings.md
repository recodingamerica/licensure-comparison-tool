### **Data-quality flags for your pipeline**

This occupation is heavily polluted by AI-generated "\[state\] HVAC Authority" sites that invent statutes wholesale. Concrete ones I hit:

* **Montana**: "montanahvacauthority.com" describes an HVAC Contractor License, HVAC Journeyperson License, an 8,000-hour requirement, and a "Board of HVAC Contractors" under MCA Title 37 — **none of which exist**. Montana has no HVAC license or board.  
* **Rhode Island**: multiple sites cite "Title 5, Chapter 5-65" for the HVAC *trade* license; the real trade license is Title 28, Ch. 28-27. Title 5/65 is a different (registration) regime.  
* **Mississippi**: aggregators cite "§ 73-59" as the contractor authority; the Board's own classification power is § 31-3-13, with 73-59 covering only the residential side.  
* **West Virginia**: one site claimed "plumbing licensing statutes under § 21-16" — but 21-16 *is* the HVAC article.

# Architect

**Architect**

All eleven states license architects — none of them lack the license. The flag in your table ("No architecture chapter found") is almost certainly a false negative from the pipeline looking for a standalone chapter titled "Architects." In most of these states, architects are regulated inside a *combined* design-professions chapter (engineers \+ architects \+ surveyors, or a "technical professions" board), so there's no chapter with "architect" in the title even though the licensure provisions are right there.

One framing note before the table: "architect" is a design-professional license, regulated separately from construction *contractor* licensing in all of these states. What's below is the architect practice/registration act, not a contractor statute.
